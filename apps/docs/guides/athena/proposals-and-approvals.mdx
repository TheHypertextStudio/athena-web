---
title: "Proposals and approvals"
description: "How an agent's work reaches you before it becomes real, and how you say yes."
---

An agent never quietly changes things on your behalf. Before it commits anything that matters, it
pauses and asks.

## A proposal looks like the thing it would become

Athena's proposals render as **translucent versions of the real thing, in the real place**. A
proposed task appears as a task row — same shape, same type, reduced opacity, a dashed border, and
a small `proposed` badge.

They are never skeletons and never greyed-out disabled states. A proposal is interactive: you can
read it, edit it, and act on it where it sits.

Two things follow from that.

**Nothing proposed can be mistaken for committed work.** "Not real yet" reads the same way at a
glance everywhere it appears — in the session, in today's plan, in a conversation.

**Approval has physical continuity.** The thing you approved is the thing that appears, in the
place you approved it. Approving does not hand you a diff and then produce something elsewhere.

## Where approvals reach you

The same pending approval appears in three places, and acting in any one resolves it:

- In the **session**, beside the work it belongs to.
- In your [**Inbox**](/guides/daily/inbox), across every workspace.
- In [**Home**](/guides/daily/home-today), inline with your plan.

You can sign off from wherever you happen to be.

Batches work the same way — a session proposing eleven changes lets you act on all of them or on
each one.

## The two dials, again

Approval behaviour comes from two independent settings.

**What an agent may touch.** Agents start **read-only** and ask for more access in the moment they
need it. Widening access is a decision you make with the reason in front of you.

**Whether actions need sign-off.**

| Mode | Behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| `suggest` | Proposes only. Nothing is ever applied without you. |
| `apply after approval` | Waits for a yes on each change, then applies it. |
| `apply directly` | Acts, and records everything for review afterwards. |

These are separate on purpose. A highly capable agent can still be held at `suggest`, and a narrow
read-only agent can be trusted to apply directly within its narrow scope.

## Who approves

By default, **whoever assigned or delegated the work**. A workspace or a team can change that, so
an organization that wants a named person signing off on agent work can have one.

## Owner and delegate

You can keep your name on a task while an agent does the work: you stay the assignee, the agent is
the **delegate**. You remain the accountable party, and the agent is the one carrying it out.

Every action records both names — the agent that did it, and the person who set it in motion.

## Suggestions are not tasks

Worth stating plainly, because it governs everything above: a suggestion has never been a task. It
becomes one at the moment you approve it, and until then it exists as a proposal.

That holds for [mail-derived suggestions](/guides/athena/athena-and-your-mail) as well as for
anything a session proposes.
