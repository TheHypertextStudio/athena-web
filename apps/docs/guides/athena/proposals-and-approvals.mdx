---
title: "Proposals and approvals"
description: "How an agent's work reaches you before it becomes real, and how you say yes."
---

An agent never quietly changes things on your behalf. Before it commits anything that matters, it
pauses and asks.

## A proposal looks like the object it would create

Athena's proposals render as **translucent copies of the real object, in the real place**. A
proposed task appears as a task row with the same shape and the same type, at reduced opacity,
with a dashed border and a small `proposed` badge.

A proposal stays interactive. You can read it, edit it, and act on it where it sits. Docket never
renders proposals as skeletons or as greyed-out disabled states.

Two results follow.

**You can tell proposed work from committed work at a glance.** A proposal reads as pending
everywhere it appears: in the session, in today's plan, in a conversation.

**Approval keeps the object where it is.** The thing you approve is the thing that appears, in the
place you approved it. Approving hands you no diff and produces nothing elsewhere.

## Where approvals reach you

The same pending approval appears in three places, and acting in any one resolves it:

- In the **session**, beside the work it belongs to.
- In your [**Inbox**](/guides/daily/inbox), across every workspace.
- In [**Home**](/guides/daily/home-today), inline with your plan.

Sign off from wherever you happen to be.

Batches work the same way. A session proposing eleven changes lets you act on all of them at once
or on each one.

## Access and sign-off

Approval behaviour comes from two independent settings.

**What an agent may touch.** Agents start **read-only** and ask for more access at the moment they
need it. You widen access with the reason in front of you.

**Whether actions need sign-off.**

| Mode | Behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| `suggest` | Proposes only, and applies nothing on its own. |
| `apply after approval` | Waits for a yes on each change, then applies it. |
| `apply directly` | Acts, and records everything for review afterwards. |

Docket keeps the two settings separate so you can hold a highly capable agent at `suggest`, or let
a narrow read-only agent apply directly within its narrow scope.

## Who approves

By default, **whoever assigned or delegated the work** approves it. A workspace or a team can
change that, so an organization can put a named person in charge of signing off on agent work.

## Owner and delegate

You can keep your name on a task while an agent does the work: you stay the assignee, and the
agent becomes the **delegate**. You remain the accountable party, and the agent carries the work
out.

Every action records both names: the agent that did it, and the person who set it in motion.

## A suggestion becomes a task only when you accept it

A suggestion has never been a task. It becomes one at the moment you approve it, and until then it
exists as a proposal. This holds for
[mail-derived suggestions](/guides/athena/athena-and-your-mail) as well as for anything a session
proposes.
