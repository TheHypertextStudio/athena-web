---
title: "What Athena is"
description: "Docket's built-in agent — a chief of staff you delegate to in plain language, and watch while she works."
---

Athena is Docket's own agent. You talk to her in plain language, hand her work the same way you
hand work to a person, and watch what she does while she does it.

Her scope is your whole plate. "Reschedule the offsite" and "create a plan to make sure I get more
sleep" are both fair things to ask.

## She belongs to you

This is the most important thing about her, and it has real consequences.

**Athena belongs to your account, never to a workspace.** A session may be *focused* on a
workspace so she knows what you are talking about, and that focus grants her no access. Every
action she takes resolves *your* identity and *your* permissions in the workspace she is acting
in.

<Note>
  Athena can never do something you could not do yourself. Approving one of her proposals cannot
  supply authority you lack. If your access changes, hers changes with it on her next action.
</Note>

The practical version: she is your assistant across every workspace you belong to, and she is not
a member of any of them.

## Agents are how work gets done

Docket treats a person and an agent as the same kind of thing — an **Actor**. Everywhere the app
asks "who", it takes either.

So delegating is not a separate mode. You assign a task to Athena in the field where you would
assign it to a colleague, on the same screen, with the same gesture. Nothing about your views
changes based on which you picked.

## Sessions are the unit

The first-class thing is not the agent. It is the **session** — one episode of an agent doing one
job.

```
You delegate a task  ─►  a session opens  ─►  you watch, steer, approve, or take over
```

A session shows a live, plain-English account of what she is reading, what she is deciding, and
what she is about to do. You can read along, nudge her when she heads the wrong way, answer a
question when she is unsure, or take the work back entirely.

The point of delegating is to watch it happen, which is also what makes it trustworthy.

## Two separate dials

Docket owns the safety model independently of how capable any agent is, and it has two dials that
move separately.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="What she may touch" icon="key">
    An agent starts **read-only** and asks for more access in the moment it needs it. Access is
    granted for a reason you can see.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Whether actions need sign-off" icon="circle-check">
    **Suggest** — proposes only. **Apply after approval** — waits for your yes.
    **Apply directly** — acts, and records everything.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

By default the approver is whoever assigned or delegated the work. A workspace or a team can
change that. See [Proposals and approvals](/guides/athena/proposals-and-approvals).

## Accountability

Every action an agent takes is attributed twice: to the agent that did it, and to the person who
set it in motion — "Athena, on behalf of you". There is never any question about who was behind a
change.

Agent effort is also recorded in the [time ledger](/guides/daily/time) alongside human effort, so
"how long did this take" has one answer regardless of who did it.

## Instructions she follows

Written guidance set at the **workspace** and **team** level applies automatically, with team
instructions overriding the workspace's.

- "Always draft, never send, without my OK."
- "Match our brand voice."
- "Never assign work to volunteers without asking me first."

Your own preferences for how she works with you live in **Settings → Athena**.

## Domain-neutral by design

A session drafting donor thank-you notes, a session updating next quarter's budget line by line,
and a session rescheduling a week of meetings all look and behave the same. Nothing about the
experience is specific to any one kind of work.
