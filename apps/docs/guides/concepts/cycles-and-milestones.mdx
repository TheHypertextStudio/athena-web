---
title: "Cycles and milestones"
description: "The two ways Docket puts dates on work: a team's recurring window and a project's dated checkpoint."
---

Both of these put a date on work, and they answer different questions.

- A **Cycle** is a stretch of time a Team works in. It belongs to the team and comes back.
- A **Milestone** is a checkpoint inside one Project. It belongs to the project and happens once.

## Cycles

A Cycle is a recurring window, commonly a week or two, that a team commits a batch of work to and
then reviews. A marketing team's content push, a monthly giving cycle, an engineering sprint.

**Cycles roll on their own.** Each team has a cadence measured in weeks, defaulting to one, and
Docket keeps a rolling window of past, current, and upcoming cycles anchored to that cadence. You
never create a cycle, close one, or remember to open the next.

Docket derives "current" from today's date instead of a stored flag, so the current cycle is
always the one you are actually in.

<Note>
  Cycles are **team-scoped**. Each Team runs its own cadence, and one team's schedule has no
  bearing on another's. Set the cadence in the team's settings.
</Note>

### Cycles schedule Tasks without containing them

A Cycle associates with Tasks. It does not own them. Pulling a task into a cycle changes when the
task is planned and leaves what the task is and where it lives alone. The task keeps its project,
its team, and its assignee.

So you can re-plan freely. Moving work between cycles is a scheduling decision, and it never
reshapes your project structure.

### What a cycle shows

The cycle view reports the work committed to the cycle against the team's capacity, plus a burn-up
of what has actually completed. A project's work list defaults to the current cycle.

## Milestones

A Milestone is a dated checkpoint that groups some of a project's tasks toward a deliverable.
"Beta to design partners." "Invitations mailed." "Venue confirmed."

Milestones are an **attribute of a Project**. They exist inside one project, they carry a date,
and they gather a subset of that project's tasks.

Add them when a project has genuine internal checkpoints that people plan around. A project with
no milestones is completely ordinary.

## Which one do I want?

| You want to say | Use |
| --- | --- |
| "This is what our team is working on for the next two weeks." | Cycle |
| "Everything before this date has to be done for the launch to work." | Milestone |
| "This work happens every month, forever." | [Program](/guides/concepts/initiatives-and-programs) |
| "This is due Thursday." | A due date on the [Task](/guides/concepts/tasks) |
