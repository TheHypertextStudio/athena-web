title: "Cycles and milestones"
description: "The two ways time attaches to work — a team's recurring window, and a project's dated checkpoint."
---

Both of these put a date on work, and they answer different questions.

- A **Cycle** is a stretch of time a team works in. It belongs to the team and comes back.
- A **Milestone** is a checkpoint inside one project. It belongs to the project and happens once.

## Cycles

A Cycle is a recurring window — commonly a week or two — that a team commits a batch of work to
and then reviews. A marketing team's content push, a monthly giving cycle, an engineering sprint.

**Cycles roll on their own.** Each team has a cadence measured in weeks, defaulting to one, and
Docket keeps a rolling window of past, current, and upcoming cycles anchored to that cadence.
There is nothing to create, nothing to close, and no ceremony to remember.

"Current" is derived from today's date rather than from a stored flag, so the current cycle is
always the one you are actually in.

<Note>
  Cycles are **team-scoped**. Each team runs its own cadence, and one team's schedule has no
  bearing on another's. Set the cadence in the team's settings.
</Note>

### Cycles schedule work; they do not contain it

A Cycle *associates* with Tasks rather than owning them. Pulling a task into a cycle changes when
it is planned, leaving what it is and where it lives alone. The same task keeps its project, its
team, and its assignee.

That separation is what lets you re-plan freely. Moving work between cycles is a scheduling
decision, so it never reshapes your project structure.

### What a cycle shows

The cycle view reports the work committed to it against the team's capacity, and a burn-up of
what has actually completed. A project list defaults to the current cycle, which is usually the
question you were about to ask.

## Milestones

A Milestone is a dated checkpoint that groups some of a project's tasks toward a deliverable.
"Beta to design partners." "Invitations mailed." "Venue confirmed."

Milestones are an **attribute of a project**, rather than a top-level concept. They exist inside
one project, they carry a date, and they gather a subset of that project's tasks.

Use them when a project has genuine internal checkpoints that people plan around. A project with
no milestones is completely ordinary.

## Which one do I want?

| You want to say | Use |
| --- | --- |
| "This is what our team is working on for the next two weeks." | Cycle |
| "Everything before this date has to be done for the launch to work." | Milestone |
| "This work happens every month, forever." | [Program](/guides/concepts/initiatives-and-programs) |
| "This is due Thursday." | A due date on the [Task](/guides/concepts/tasks) |
