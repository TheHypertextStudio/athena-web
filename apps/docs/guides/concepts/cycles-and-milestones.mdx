- Use **Cycles** to plan the next week or two your Team works in. They come back.
- Use a **Milestone** to mark one date inside one Project. It happens once.

## Cycles

Use Cycles to plan work in recurring sprints. The marketing team's content push. The monthly giving
cycle. An engineering sprint.

**Cycles roll on their own.** Each team picks its length in the team's settings, one week by
default. Docket rolls past, current, and upcoming cycles on that cadence and works out "current"
from today's date. You never create a cycle or close one.

<Note>
  Set the cadence per Team. Marketing can run one week and engineering two.
</Note>

### Cycles schedule Tasks without containing them

Pull a task into a cycle to change when it is planned. It keeps its project, its team, and its
assignee. Move work between cycles as often as you like; you never reshape the project structure by
doing it.

A cycle reports the work committed to it against the team's capacity, plus a burn-up of what has
completed. A project's work list defaults to the current cycle.

## Milestones

Add a Milestone to a Project to name a date and gather the tasks that have to be done by it. "Beta
to design partners." "Invitations mailed." "Venue confirmed."

Milestones are an **attribute of a Project**. Add them when the project has checkpoints people plan
around.

## Cycle, Milestone, or Program

| You want to say | Use |
| --- | --- |
| "This is what our team is working on for the next two weeks." | Cycle |
| "Everything before this date has to be done for the launch to work." | Milestone |
| "This work happens every month, forever." | [Program](/guides/concepts/initiatives-and-programs) |
| "This is due Thursday." | A due date on the [Task](/guides/concepts/tasks) |
