title: "Labels and statuses"
description: "Your workspace's own words for how far along work is, and for everything else you need to say about it."
---

Two workspace-owned vocabularies, doing different jobs. A **status** answers "how far along is
this?" A **label** is everything else you want to say.

## Statuses

A status is a stage work moves through. Your workspace names its own: how many there are, what
they are called, what each one means, what order they read in, and which one new work starts in.

A grant-making nonprofit can run `Drafting → Submitted → Under review → Awarded`. A studio can run
`Idea → Booked → Shot → Delivered`. Each is describing its real process in its own words.

Set them up in **Settings → Statuses**.

### The five categories

Every status a workspace invents declares one of five categories. That much is Docket's, and it is
what makes the rest yours.

| Category | Means |
| --- | --- |
| `backlog` | Recorded, not queued. |
| `unstarted` | Queued, not begun. |
| `started` | In progress. |
| `completed` | Finished successfully. |
| `canceled` | Ended without being finished. |

The category is what draws a status glyph, what a board groups by, what a progress percentage
counts, what a connector maps a Linear or Jira state onto, and what tells Docket that a piece of
work just finished.

Two workspaces calling the same stage `Shipped` and `Awarded` are both saying `completed`, so
their numbers, their icons, and their integrations still line up.

<Note>
  This is also what agents rely on. An agent comparing work across two teams reads the category
  rather than the label, so it never has to learn your words to know that something is done.
</Note>

### Statuses are per team and per kind of work

A team owns the workflow states its Tasks move through, so two teams in the same workspace can run
genuinely different processes.

Projects, Programs, and Initiatives carry their own status sets, because "how far along is this
project" and "how far along is this task" are not the same question. The seeded Program set is
`Proposed · Active · Paused · Completed · Archived`.

## Labels

A label is free-form workspace vocabulary that you attach to work. `needs-legal`. `Q3`.
`grant-funded`. `flaky`.

Labels carry no behaviour. They do not gate anything, order anything, or mean anything to Docket
beyond being a thing you can filter and search on. That is the point — statuses have rules, and
labels are where you put everything that should not.

Manage them in **Settings → Labels**, described there as "the words this workspace uses that
Docket does not ship."

## Which one do I reach for?

| You want to record | Use |
| --- | --- |
| That this is in review | A **status** |
| That this is blocked on someone outside the company | A **label**, plus a comment naming who |
| That this belongs to the Q3 push | An [Initiative](/guides/concepts/initiatives-and-programs), or a label if it is lighter than that |
| That this was cancelled rather than finished | A **status** in the `canceled` category |
| That this needs a lawyer to look at it | A **label** |

<Tip>
  Keep the status set short. Every status is a decision someone has to make every time they touch
  a piece of work, and a seven-stage workflow usually means two of the stages are labels wearing a
  disguise.
</Tip>
