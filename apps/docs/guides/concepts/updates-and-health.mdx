title: "Updates and health"
description: "How you say what is really going on, and how that becomes a signal other people can read at a glance."
---

An **Update** is a narrative status post on a Project, Program, or Initiative. You write what is
actually happening in your own words, and you set a verdict alongside it.

That verdict becomes the item's **health**, which is what everyone else reads from
[Portfolio](/guides/daily/portfolio) without opening anything.

## The three verdicts

| Health | Means |
| --- | --- |
| `on_track` | It is going the way it should. |
| `at_risk` | Something is threatening it. Nothing has gone wrong yet. |
| `off_track` | It has already slipped. |

Health is deliberately something a person asserts. Docket does not infer it from ticket counts or
burn-down slope, because a project can be 80% complete and doomed, or 20% complete and completely
fine.

## Why health sits beside progress rather than replacing it

A Project reports **weighted progress** as well as health. The two answer different questions:

- Progress: how much of the work is done.
- Health: whether the remaining work is going to land.

A reader needs both. A program has no meaningful progress percentage at all — it is designed to
keep going — so health and flow are the entire signal there.

## Writing a good update

<Steps>
  <Step title="Say what changed">
    Since the last update. Not since the beginning.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Say what is in the way">
    Name the blocker and who it belongs to. Anything you are waiting on is worth stating plainly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the verdict honestly">
    An `at_risk` posted early is useful. An `on_track` posted the week before a slip is the thing
    that erodes trust in the whole signal.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Updates are for the people who are *not* in the work every day. Write for the reader who last
  looked a fortnight ago.
</Tip>

## Rolled-up health

An Initiative reports health rolled up from the Programs and Projects associated with it, so a
theme going wrong is visible from the top without anyone assembling a report.

That roll-up is why an honest `off_track` on a single project matters beyond the project — it is
load-bearing for everything above it.

## Where updates show up

- On the item itself, as a chronological record of how it went.
- In [Portfolio](/guides/daily/portfolio), as the health chip on every row.
- In the [Inbox](/guides/daily/inbox) of people following the item.
- On a published **brief**, if the project is published — which is exactly what a brief is for.

## Attention signals

Initiatives surface their own attention flags when something needs looking at: `off_track`,
`at_risk`, or **stale** — meaning nobody has posted an update in long enough that the health
signal can no longer be trusted.

Staleness being a first-class signal is deliberate. Silence reads as "fine" in most tools, and it
usually is not.
