title: "Workspaces and your Hub"
description: "The two altitudes Docket is built on — the personal Hub that spans everything, and the isolated workspace where work actually lives."
---

Docket has exactly two heights. Almost every question about where something lives resolves to
which one you are standing at.

```
   YOU (one person)
     │ owns
     ▼
   ┌──────────────────── YOUR HUB ────────────────────┐
   │  Home · Inbox · Portfolio · Search               │
   │  spans every workspace you belong to             │
   └───────┬──────────────────────────┬───────────────┘
           │ owns                     │ gathers, via your membership
           ▼                          ▼
   ┌───────────────┐      ┌───────────────┐   ┌───────────────┐
   │ Personal space│      │  Workspace    │   │  Workspace    │
   │  (yours only) │      │  "Startup"    │   │  "Nonprofit"  │
   └───────────────┘      └───────────────┘   └───────────────┘
```

## Your Hub

One per person, and it belongs to you rather than to any organization. The Hub aggregates across
every workspace you are a member of and owns your cross-workspace surfaces:

- [**Home**](/guides/daily/home-today) — today's plan, your calendar, and what needs attention.
- [**Inbox**](/guides/daily/inbox) — everything awaiting a response, from every workspace.
- [**Portfolio**](/guides/daily/portfolio) — every workspace's programs and projects on one
  timeline.
- [**Search**](/guides/daily/search-and-views) — one palette that fuses search, navigation,
  actions, and switching workspaces.

Every item pulled into a Hub surface wears a chip naming the workspace it came from.

## A workspace

The boundary that isolates one venture from another. Scoped to a workspace and invisible outside
it: its members, its connected tools, its agents, its
[terminology](/guides/concepts/terminology), and all of its work.

Drop into a workspace and the whole app rebinds — it speaks that workspace's language and shows
its people and its tools. Step back to the Hub and you are aggregated again.

<Note>
  **Separation is structural. Unification is a view.** Data never merges across workspaces;
  Hub surfaces gather. The same workspace appears in every member's own Hub, because a workspace
  is a shared tenant rather than something your Hub privately owns.
</Note>

## Your personal space

An organization of one, for work tied to no venture. It reuses the same machinery as a shared
workspace, so tasks, projects, cycles, and Athena all work identically inside it.

What it drops is the parts that only make sense with other people in the room: there is no member
roster to manage, no publishing settings, and its data sources are your personal connections
rather than a separate shared set.

Docket creates it for you when you sign up as an individual. You never name it, and you are never
asked to invent an organization to hold your own to-do list.

## Membership, roles, and access

Access inside a workspace is decided by two things working together.

**Capabilities** are a single ordered ladder. A higher one satisfies every lower one.

| Capability | What it allows |
| --- | --- |
| `view` | Read the item. |
| `comment` | Add comments and discussion. |
| `contribute` | Create and edit content — tasks, fields. |
| `assign` | Route and assign work to others. |
| `manage` | Full control, including settings, membership, and deletion. |

**Grants** bind a subject to a place in the work tree. The subject is either one Actor or an
entire role, and the target is any node from the workspace down to a single task —
organization, team, initiative, program, project, cycle, or task. A grant cascades down
containment, so `contribute` on a Program reaches the Projects and Tasks inside it.

An item is also either **public** to workspace members or **private**, in which case only holders
of an explicit grant can see it at all.

Roles themselves are workspace-defined. You name them and pick what they carry, so a nonprofit's
"Volunteer" and an agency's "Client" can both be real roles rather than an approximation of
someone else's fixed list.

Manage all of this in **Settings → Members & Access**.

## Switching, adding, and leaving

The workspace switcher sits at the top of the sidebar. Creating a workspace opens a focused page
asking for a name and a terminology preset.

Every workspace you join appears in your Hub automatically, and leaving one removes it from your
Hub while leaving the workspace itself untouched for everyone else.
