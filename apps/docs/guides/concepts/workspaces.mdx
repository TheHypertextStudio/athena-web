title: "Workspaces and your Hub"
description: "The two levels Docket is built on: the personal Hub that spans everything, and the isolated workspace where work lives."
---

Docket has exactly two levels. Almost every question about where something lives comes down to
which of the two you mean.

```
   YOU (one person)
     │ owns
     ▼
   ┌──────────────────── YOUR HUB ────────────────────┐
   │  Home · Inbox · Portfolio · Search               │
   │  spans every workspace you belong to             │
   └───────┬──────────────────────────┬───────────────┘
           │ owns                     │ gathers, via your membership
           ▼                          ▼
   ┌───────────────┐      ┌───────────────┐   ┌───────────────┐
   │ Personal space│      │  Workspace    │   │  Workspace    │
   │  (yours only) │      │  "Startup"    │   │  "Nonprofit"  │
   └───────────────┘      └───────────────┘   └───────────────┘
```

## Your Hub

You get one Hub, and you own it personally. No organization owns it. The Hub aggregates across
every workspace you are a member of and owns your cross-workspace surfaces:

- [**Home**](/guides/daily/home-today): today's plan, your calendar, and what needs attention.
- [**Inbox**](/guides/daily/inbox): everything awaiting a response, from every workspace.
- [**Portfolio**](/guides/daily/portfolio): every workspace's programs and projects on one
  timeline.
- [**Search**](/guides/daily/search-and-views): one palette that fuses search, navigation,
  actions, and switching workspaces.

Every item pulled into a Hub surface shows a chip naming the workspace it came from.

## A workspace

A workspace is the boundary that isolates one venture from another. Its members, its connected
tools, its agents, its [terminology](/guides/concepts/terminology), and all of its work are scoped
to it and invisible outside it.

Open a workspace and the whole app switches to it, showing that workspace's terminology, people,
and tools. Return to the Hub to see everything gathered together again.

<Note>
  Data never merges across workspaces. Hub surfaces gather it for display. The same workspace
  appears in every member's own Hub, because a workspace is a shared tenant that no single Hub
  owns.
</Note>

## Your personal space

Your personal space is an organization of one, for work tied to no venture. It uses the same
features as a shared workspace, so tasks, projects, cycles, and Athena all work identically
inside it.

It leaves out the parts that only apply with other people involved. There is no member roster to
manage and no publishing settings, and it draws its data sources from your personal connections.

Docket creates your personal space when you sign up as an individual. You never name it, and you
are never asked to invent an organization to hold your own to-do list.

## Membership, roles, and access

Two things together decide access inside a workspace.

**Capabilities** form a single ordered list. A higher one satisfies every lower one.

| Capability | What it allows |
| --- | --- |
| `view` | Read the item. |
| `comment` | Add comments and discussion. |
| `contribute` | Create and edit content such as tasks and fields. |
| `assign` | Route and assign work to others. |
| `manage` | Full control, including settings, membership, and deletion. |

**Grants** bind a subject to a place in the work tree. The subject is either one Actor or an
entire role. The target is any node from the workspace down to a single task: organization, team,
initiative, program, project, cycle, or task. A grant cascades down containment, so `contribute`
on a Program reaches the Projects and Tasks inside it.

An item is also either **public** to workspace members or **private**, in which case only holders
of an explicit grant can see it at all.

Each workspace defines its own roles. You name a role and pick what it carries, so a nonprofit can
create "Volunteer" and an agency can create "Client".

Manage all of this in **Settings → Members & Access**.

## Switching, adding, and leaving

The workspace switcher sits at the top of the sidebar. Creating a workspace opens a focused page
asking for a name and a terminology preset.

Every workspace you join appears in your Hub automatically. Leaving one removes it from your Hub
and leaves the workspace itself untouched for everyone else.
