Docket's calendar holds your meetings and your work in the same grid. A task with a duration can
be placed on it like anything else, which is what keeps the plan and the week from being two
different documents that disagree.

## Layers

The calendar is layered. Each source — your Google calendar, a workspace's shared schedule, your
own scheduled work — is a layer you can show, hide, and share independently.

Layers can be shared with other people, so a team can see the shape of your week without seeing
the contents of every event in it.

## Two-way Google sync

Connect Google Calendar and its events appear as ordinary rows here. Work you schedule in Docket
is written back to Google, so the rest of your life sees the time you have committed.

Event times preserve the source calendar's own zone, so nothing drifts when you and the calendar
disagree about where you are.

See [Connect your calendar and mail](/guides/connect-calendar-and-mail).

## Scheduling a week

Docket can lay out a week for you from the work you have and the time you actually have free.

The important part of how it does this: **time is not fungible.** Filming a session, sitting in a
community meeting, writing something long, reading on a bus, and debriefing after an event are
five qualitatively different kinds of time. A scheduler that treats them as interchangeable
cheerfully puts a three-hour shoot into a 25-minute bus ride, produces a week that is technically
full, and gets abandoned on day two.

So the kind of time is part of the model. Work is placed into time that suits it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Estimates come first">
    Scheduling reads each task's [time estimate](/guides/concepts/tasks). Unestimated work cannot
    be placed honestly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Your real calendar is the constraint">
    Existing meetings and events are fixed. Scheduling fills what is left.
  </Step>
  <Step title="You approve the week">
    The result is a proposal you accept, edit, or reject — the same
    [approval model](/guides/athena/proposals-and-approvals) everything else uses.
  </Step>
  <Step title="It learns from what actually happened">
    The [time ledger](/guides/daily/time) records how long work really took, which is what makes
    the next week's estimates better than the last.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The daily loop

Weekly scheduling sets the shape; [Home](/guides/daily/home-today) adjusts it each morning against
what actually happened yesterday. Neither replaces the other — the week is a plan, and the day is
where the plan meets reality.

## Scheduling defaults

Working hours, default durations, and how your calendar is shared live in
**Settings → Calendar**.
