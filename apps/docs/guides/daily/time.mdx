---
title: "Time"
description: "Track the hours your work takes and compare them to the hours you planned."
---

Start a timer on a task and Docket records the exact minutes. Pause it, resume it, move it to
different work, or stop it.

Docket writes each run into the **time ledger**: started 9:14, stopped 10:02, and who was working.
Dragging a task to `completed` adds no hours. Neither does a one-hour event on Thursday's
calendar.

## What you can track

- A [Task](/guides/concepts/tasks)
- A calendar item
- A whole workspace, project, or category
- Freeform activity that maps to nothing in Docket
- An Athena session, or a subagent inside one

Athena's hours land in the same ledger as yours.

## Move the timer to different work

Move the timer from the grant draft to the 2pm call and Docket closes the first interval as it
opens the second. You get two intervals.

## Planned against actual

| | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- |
| **Planned** | The [time estimate](/guides/concepts/tasks) on the task |
| **Scheduled** | Where [scheduling](/guides/daily/calendar-and-scheduling) placed it |
| **Actual** | The time ledger |

Estimate an hour, spend two, every week: that pattern shows up here, and next week's scheduling
reads it.

## Submitting hours to a client or a funder

Submit hours to one recipient at a time. Send a client their hours and a funder theirs from the
same record, and neither sees the other's scope.

## What Docket will not do

- Invent time you did not track.
- Derive an interval from a status change.
- Treat a calendar event as proof you were doing the thing.

<Tip>
  Track a week before you change anything. The first useful result is often that the Monday sync
  costs three hours, not one.
</Tip>
