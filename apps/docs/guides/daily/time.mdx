---
title: "Time"
description: "How Docket records the hours your work actually took, and how they compare to the hours you planned."
---

Docket keeps a **time ledger**: a record of the effort you chose to track. Every entry has a real
actor and concrete start and end instants, so a tracked interval is a measurement.

The ledger stays separate from everything that merely implies time. A task moving to `completed`,
an event sitting on your calendar, and an agent finishing a session are signals that time passed,
and none of them measures it. Docket never substitutes one of them for a tracked interval.

## What you can track

- A [Task](/guides/concepts/tasks)
- A calendar item
- A whole workspace, project, or category
- Freeform activity that maps to nothing in Docket
- An Athena session, or a subagent inside one

Docket records agent work in the same ledger as human work, so "how long did this actually take"
has one answer regardless of who did it.

## Starting and stopping

Start a timer from the work itself: a task, a calendar item, a project. There is no separate
stopwatch screen to remember.

Pause, resume, switch to different work, or stop a running timer at any point. Switching records
the boundary between the two pieces of work, so you end up with two accurate intervals.

## Planned against actual

| | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- |
| **Planned** | The [time estimate](/guides/concepts/tasks) on the task |
| **Scheduled** | Where [scheduling](/guides/daily/calendar-and-scheduling) placed it |
| **Actual** | The time ledger |

Read the three together to see how good your estimates are. Estimates that consistently come in at
half of reality show up here, and that history feeds next week's plan.

## Reflection and reporting

Tracked time backs a personal view of where your hours went, plus workspace-level reporting for
teams that need it.

Submissions are **recipient-scoped**: you submit time to whoever needs it, so a client, a
grant-reporting requirement, and your own reflection draw from the same record without seeing each
other's scope.

## What Docket will not do

- Invent time you did not track.
- Derive an interval from a status change.
- Treat a calendar event as proof you were doing the thing.

You elect to track, per piece of work.

<Tip>
  Track a week before you change anything. The first useful result is often the discovery that one
  recurring commitment costs three times what you assumed.
</Tip>
