title: "Time"
description: "The time ledger — a record of the hours work actually took, kept beside the hours you planned."
---

Docket keeps a **time ledger**: a record of effort you chose to track, with a real actor and
concrete start and end instants. A tracked interval is a measured fact.

The ledger stays deliberately separate from everything that merely *implies* time. A task moving
to `completed`, an event sitting on your calendar, and an agent finishing a session are all
signals about time, and none of them is a measurement. Docket declines to substitute any of them
for the ledger.

## What you can track

- A [Task](/guides/concepts/tasks)
- A calendar item
- A whole workspace, project, or category
- Freeform activity that maps to nothing in Docket
- An Athena session, or a subagent inside one

That last pair matters: agent work is accounted in the same ledger as human work, so "how long did
this actually take" has one answer regardless of who did it.

## Starting and stopping

Timers start from the work itself — a task, a calendar item, a project — rather than from a
separate stopwatch screen you have to remember exists.

A running timer can be paused, resumed, switched to different work, or stopped. Switching records
the boundary rather than losing it, so moving between two tasks leaves two honest intervals
instead of one vague one.

## Planned against actual

This is what the ledger is for.

| | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- |
| **Planned** | The [time estimate](/guides/concepts/tasks) on the task |
| **Scheduled** | Where [scheduling](/guides/daily/calendar-and-scheduling) placed it |
| **Actual** | The ledger |

Reading the three together tells you whether your estimates are any good — which is the input that
makes next week's plan better. Estimates that are consistently half of reality are worth knowing
about, and no amount of planning discipline surfaces that on its own.

## Reflection and reporting

The ledger backs a personal view of where your hours went, and workspace-level reporting for teams
that need it.

Submissions are **recipient-scoped**: time is submitted to whoever needs it, so a client, a
grant-reporting requirement, and your own reflection can draw from the same ledger without seeing
each other's scope.

## What Docket will not do

- It will not invent time you did not track.
- It will not derive an interval from a status change.
- It will not treat a calendar event as proof you were doing the thing.

Tracking is something you elect to do, per piece of work.

<Tip>
  Track a week honestly before changing anything. The first useful output of a time ledger is
  usually the discovery that one recurring commitment costs three times what you assumed.
</Tip>
