title: "Your first week"
description: "The daily loop: plan the morning, work the day, review the week."
---

Docket runs on a daily loop. Once you know it, most days need about five minutes of deliberate
planning.

## Every morning: build the plan

Open [Home](/guides/daily/home-today). It shows three things side by side: the plan you are
building for today, your calendar, and everything that needs your attention.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pull in what matters">
    Drag tasks from any workspace into today's plan. Every one shows a chip naming where it came
    from, so a nonprofit task sitting beside a startup task stays clearly labelled.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Give each one a duration">
    A task carries an estimate of how long it will take. Durations let you compare the plan
    against the hours you actually have.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Clear what is blocking you">
    Blockers, agent approvals, and overdue items appear inline in the plan, so you never have to
    remember to visit a separate queue.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Or ask Athena">
    "What should I focus on today?" She reads the same work you can see and proposes a plan you
    approve or edit.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Through the day: do the work

Scheduled work sits on your [calendar](/guides/daily/calendar-and-scheduling) as ordinary rows
beside your meetings. Start a timer on a task and Docket records the interval against it, so the
plan, the calendar, and the [time ledger](/guides/daily/time) all agree.

Anything that needs a response, such as a mention, an agent waiting on approval, or a comment,
lands in your [Inbox](/guides/daily/inbox), which spans every workspace you belong to.

## Through the week: cycles, updates, and triage

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Cycles set the cadence">
    Each team runs its own recurring stretch of time, commonly two weeks. Cycles roll forward on
    their own schedule, so there is no ceremony to remember and no cycle to create by hand. The
    timeline shows past, current, and upcoming. See
    [Cycles and milestones](/guides/concepts/cycles-and-milestones).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Updates report progress">
    Post an update on a project, program, or initiative to say how it is actually going. The
    update drives that item's health signal, which a reader sees from
    [Portfolio](/guides/daily/portfolio) without opening anything.
    See [Updates and health](/guides/concepts/updates-and-health).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Triage holds unfiled work">
    Some work arrives unfiled, such as a task from a connected tool or something you captured in
    a hurry. It waits in Triage until you file it. A task is allowed to have no
    project, so you can record a thought without inventing structure for it.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## End of the week: look back

[Portfolio](/guides/daily/portfolio) puts every workspace's programs and projects on one timeline,
so you can see what moved and what did not.

The [time ledger](/guides/daily/time) shows where the hours actually went, next to what you
planned. Athena can write the retrospective for you if you would rather read one than assemble it.

## A reasonable first week

| Day | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Connect a calendar and one work tool. Let real work land in the workspace. |
| 1 | Create one Project for something you are actually doing this month. |
| 2 | Plan a morning from Home. Give three tasks a duration and see how the day holds. |
| 3 | Delegate one task to Athena and watch the session. Approve or reject what she proposes. |
| 4 | Post an update on the Project. Notice the health signal change in Portfolio. |
| 5 | Read the time ledger against the plan. Adjust your estimates. |

<Tip>
  You do not need to model everything on day one. Docket accepts a Task with no project, so you
  can record work now and give it structure when the structure is obvious.
</Tip>
