Docket has a rhythm. Once it clicks, most days need about five minutes of deliberate planning.

## Every morning: build the plan

Open [Home](/guides/daily/home-today). It shows three things side by side: the plan you are
building for today, your calendar, and everything that needs your attention.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pull in what matters">
    Drag tasks from any workspace into today's plan. Every one wears a chip naming where it came
    from, so a nonprofit task sitting beside a startup task never gets confused for one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Give each one a duration">
    A task carries an estimate of how long it will take. That estimate is what lets the plan sit
    honestly against the hours you actually have, instead of becoming a wish list.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Clear what is blocking you">
    Blockers, agent approvals, and overdue items appear inline in the plan rather than in a
    separate queue you have to remember to visit.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Or ask Athena">
    "What should I focus on today?" She reads the same work you can see and proposes a plan you
    approve or edit.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Through the day: do the work

Scheduled work sits on your [calendar](/guides/daily/calendar-and-scheduling) as ordinary rows
beside your meetings. Start a timer on a task and Docket records the interval against it — the
plan, the calendar, and the [time ledger](/guides/daily/time) end up being one record rather than
three that disagree.

Anything that needs a response — a mention, an agent waiting on approval, a comment — lands in
your [Inbox](/guides/daily/inbox), which spans every workspace you belong to.

## Through the week: keep the shape

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Cycles carry the cadence">
    Each team runs its own recurring stretch of time — commonly two weeks. Cycles roll forward on
    their own schedule, so there is no ceremony to remember and no cycle to create by hand. The
    timeline shows past, current, and upcoming. See
    [Cycles and milestones](/guides/concepts/cycles-and-milestones).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Updates carry the story">
    Post an update on a project, program, or initiative to say how it is actually going. The
    update drives that item's health signal, which is what a reader sees from
    [Portfolio](/guides/daily/portfolio) without opening anything.
    See [Updates and health](/guides/concepts/updates-and-health).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Triage catches the rest">
    Work arrives without a home — a task from a connected tool, something you captured in a
    hurry. It waits in Triage until you file it. A task is allowed to have no project, so nothing
    forces you to invent structure to record a thought.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## End of the week: look back

[Portfolio](/guides/daily/portfolio) puts every workspace's programs and projects on one timeline,
which is the fastest way to see what moved and what quietly did not.

The [time ledger](/guides/daily/time) shows where the hours actually went, next to what you
planned. Athena can write the retrospective for you if you would rather read one than assemble it.

## A reasonable first week

| Day | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Connect a calendar and one work tool. Let real work land in the workspace. |
| 1 | Create one Project for something you are actually doing this month. |
| 2 | Plan a morning from Home. Give three tasks a duration and see how the day holds. |
| 3 | Delegate one task to Athena and watch the session. Approve or reject what she proposes. |
| 4 | Post an update on the Project. Notice the health signal change in Portfolio. |
| 5 | Read the time ledger against the plan. Adjust your estimates, not your ambitions. |

<Tip>
  Skip the urge to model everything on day one. A Task with no project is a first-class citizen in
  Docket, so you can record work now and give it structure when the structure is obvious.
</Tip>
